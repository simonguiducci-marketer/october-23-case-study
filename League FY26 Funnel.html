The recommendation in two minutes

    The H1 FY27 plan

    Balanced plan, Syndication Option A, 25% diminishing returns as the base case. Test other mixes in the Budget allocator tab.

    Channel caps
    • Caps are per half-year (data package README), so the plan uses them as stated rather than doubling.
    • Webinars: at most 3 a month at about $3K each, so $54K a half (about $108K a year).
    • Demandbase: about $40K a half across the 1,200 Tier 1 accounts (about $80K a year).
    • FY26 ran below both caps, at about $36K and $32.5K a half.
    • The README treats the Demandbase limit as soft (past it you buy frequency, not reach). The plan holds it as a hard cap because spend beyond it reaches no new accounts.

    What has to be true

    LinkedIn tracking is fixed before it scales

    35 LinkedIn leads lost their UTMs during the Feb–Apr landing page migration. LinkedIn takes the largest share of the plan, so its results must be measurable from July.

    MQLs are gated on company size

    Leads at 1,000+ employees convert to SQL at 20%, smaller ones at 6%. Add the gate to Pardot scoring and paid targeting.

    Sales follows up every SQL

    35 of 105 FY26 SQLs had no owner. Agree routing and a follow-up SLA, and report owner coverage weekly.

    Webinars run three a month

    The plan funds Webinars to their cap. FY26 ran about two a month, so this is a production commitment, not just a budget line.

    HLTH 2026 earns its $45K

    Attach executive roundtables, which closed 3 of the 4 Events wins. HLTH is mid-November, so its leads must reach SQL by early December to count in H1.

    Programs are front-loaded

    MQL to SQL takes about 3 weeks and MQL to won about 5.5 months. Spend weighted to July–October counts in H1; revenue lands in H2 FY27.

    Alternatives considered

    Same $280K, same assumptions. SQL range runs from 50% diminishing returns to none.

      What this means for H1 FY27
      The Tier 1 flag can't be trusted as it stands

      148 of the 376 Tier 1 leads (39%) are at companies under 1,000 employees, below League's ICP, and 4 Demandbase leads are flagged "No" (LD-13). An enterprise target list shouldn't look like this.

      Rebuild the flag by matching lead domains to the Demandbase account list before using it to judge ABM.
      As flagged, Tier 1 barely outperforms

      Tier 1 leads convert to SQL at 16% against 13% for the rest, with 9 wins against 7 and almost the same ACV per win (about $730K).

      Re-tier accounts on fit (size, segment) and intent, so the tier predicts something Sales can act on.
      The channel matters more than the flag

      Tier 1 leads from Events convert at 39% and from Demandbase at 32%. Tier 1 leads bought through Syndication convert at 5% and through Google Demand Gen at 3%.

      Reach Tier 1 accounts through Events, roundtables, Demandbase and LinkedIn, not through Syndication or Google Demand Gen.
      One contact per account is not ABM

      376 of the 1,200 Tier 1 accounts (31%) produced a lead, and every one produced exactly one. Enterprise deals here involve several decision-makers.

      Measure ABM on accounts engaged and contacts per account, and use Demandbase to widen buying-committee coverage, not lead count.

      What this means for H1 FY27
      Deals are lost on qualification, not to competitors

      Only 1 of the 10 losses went to a competitor. The other 9 are fit, timing, budget or no decision.

      Tighten qualification at SQL acceptance with Sales; the problem sits upstream of the deal, not in competitive positioning.
      The "company size" loss reason is mislabelled

      3 of the 4 "Not a fit: company size" losses are companies with 5,000+ employees. Only 1 is under 1,000.

      Agree loss-reason definitions with Sales (too small, too complex, wrong buyer) before using them to steer targeting.
      The very largest accounts stall

      Companies with 20,000+ employees closed 0 of 4: no budget, no decision, and two "not a fit". Companies with 1,000 to 19,999 closed 14 of 18.

      Keep 1,000–19,999 as the core paid target; treat 20,000+ as an ABM motion with executive sponsorship, not volume.
      Some of H1 FY27's pipeline already exists

      Two losses were marked "Timing: revisit FY27", and 26 SQOs were still open at the 30 Jun 2026 extract.

      Run a re-engagement program with AEs on these accounts before buying new volume; they are the cheapest SQLs available.

      What this means for H1 FY27

      The offer matters inside a channel

      On LinkedIn, the Total Cost of Care report converts to SQL at 27%; the Benefits Leader ABM campaign at 15%. The AI in Member Experience webinar beats Navigating Care Costs, 31% to 25%. Both Google Demand Gen campaigns sit at 2–3%.

      Shift LinkedIn toward the Total Cost of Care report and AI-led content, and rebuild or retire the Benefits Leader ABM campaign.
      Practitioners convert; executives rarely do through forms

      Benefits Managers convert at 28% and Directors of Total Rewards at 22%, with 7 of the 16 wins ($5.5M). SVP HR, VP People Ops and VP Total Rewards convert at 8–10%, with 1 win between them.

      Aim paid offers at Total Rewards and Benefits directors and managers; reach VPs and the C-suite through ABM and roundtables.
      Canada converts at about half the US rate

      Canada produced 154 MQLs but only 9% reached SQL, with 1 win. US regions ran at 13–19%. The real League says 1 in 2 Canadians use its platform, so paid demand may matter less there (inference).

      Weight H1 FY27 paid media toward the US, after checking with Sales how Canadian deals actually arrive.
      SQLs arrive in weeks; revenue takes half a year

      Median: 10 days from form fill to MQL, about 3 weeks from MQL to SQL, and about 5.5 months from MQL to a won deal. The SQL lag is similar on every channel.

      Judge H1 FY27 on SQLs, not revenue. Front-load programs in July to October, since MQLs after early December land as SQLs in the next half.

      What the plan delivers

      Allocation

      Content Syndication: three options at the August renewal

      Option A: complete the contract, then cancel (default)

      Pay out the current term through July (about $8,125) and do not renew. FY26: 154 unique leads against 200 guaranteed, 10% to SQL, $6,500 per SQL.

      Frees about $40K for channels that convert. Risk: losing a source of mid-funnel volume if LinkedIn doesn't scale.
      Option B: renegotiate on terms that match FY26 results

      Pay only for unique leads at 1,000+ employee companies, de-duplicate before delivery, credit the 46-lead shortfall, and add a monthly exit clause. Price and volume are assumptions you can type in below the table.

      1,000+ Syndication leads converted at only 10% in FY26, so price decides it. At about $600 per unique lead or less, it beats LinkedIn's extra spend; above that, Option A wins.
      Option C: renew on current terms

      Keep the $97,500-a-year contract ($48,750 this half) as it is: 200 guaranteed leads, no 1,000+ filter and no de-duplication. In FY26 it delivered 154 unique leads, 10% to SQL.

      Roughly SQL-neutral (within half an SQL) and about 35 more MQLs, whenever the money would otherwise go to LinkedIn above its FY26 level. The cost: rewarding a vendor that missed its guarantee by 23%, with no leverage left.
      Assumptions (editable)

      Cleaned FY26 rates, full year. Change a value to test it; "Reset" restores FY26. SQLs = MQLs × MQL → SQL rate. SQOs, wins and ACV use the FY26 averages: 49.5% SQL → SQO, 30.8% SQO → Won, $734K average ACV.

      How credit is assigned

      The extract uses single-touch, lead-level attribution: each lead counts for the one campaign it was assigned to, which is the campaign behind its form fill. There is no touchpoint history, campaign-member data or opportunity contact roles, so multi-touch credit cannot be rebuilt from this package.

      What it distorts

      Awareness and ABM channels (Demandbase display, LinkedIn retargeting) influence deals without getting the form fill, so their cost per SQL is likely overstated. Direct-response channels look relatively better. Demandbase's cost per SQL is the number most exposed.

      Make decisions that hold under any model: Google Demand Gen's own leads convert at 2%, and the efficient channels are capped.
      What would fix it

      Salesforce campaign-member history, opportunity contact roles, Demandbase account-level engagement and a self-reported "how did you hear about us" field. The Tier 1 flag and form type can't stand in for these (LD-12, LD-13).

      Put these in place in the first 90 days, and run a holdout test on part of the Tier 1 list to measure Demandbase directly.

      What I chose to ignore

      • Form type (LD-12): every form type appears on every channel, so it carries no signal.
      • Impressions and clicks (SP-07): only two of six channels report them.
      • Monthly spend trends for Syndication, Demandbase and Google Demand Gen (SP-03): the amounts are straight-lined.
      • Campaign-level costs (SP-04): four channels report spend rolled up, so costs stay at channel level.
      • Channel win rates and Media CAC for ranking: 0 to 4 wins per channel is too thin.
      • Job titles as a targeting rule: titles spread evenly across segments, likely a synthetic-data artefact.
      • Leads created before FY26 or MQL'd after the extract (LD-07, LD-08): kept and flagged; too few to matter.
